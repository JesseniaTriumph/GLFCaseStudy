# What our grantees tell us blocks credential completion

Synthesis across nine workforce grantees reporting through FY25.

Four barriers recur:

1. **Income pressure during training.** The most consistent theme. Participants leave
   programs to return to paid work. Where grantees raised stipends, completion rose
   materially (one grantee moved from 61% to 74% between cohorts).

2. **Transportation and childcare.** Named by most grantees as day-to-day friction that
   compounds the income-pressure problem.

3. **Employer recognition of non-degree credentials.** Several grantees found employer
   partners applied the credential inconsistently in hiring. The ones making progress
   are co-designing curriculum with a small set of anchor employers.

4. **The graduation-to-first-role gap.** Completion is necessary but not sufficient —
   the transition into a paid role is where the earnings effect is won or lost. See the
   AkiraChix site-visit notes for a clear example.

The credential-completion thesis tag currently covers 11 organizations; 7 have reported
outcomes. Three Colombia grantees report in Spanish and are under-represented in this memo.
